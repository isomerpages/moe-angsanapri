<p><strong>Applied Learning Programme (ALP)</strong>
</p>
<p><strong>Developing&nbsp;21st Century Competencies Through Design Thinking for Sustainability</strong>
</p>
<p>At Angsana Primary School, sustainability education is more than just
learning about the environment. It is a way for students to build the essential
skills they need for the future.</p>
<ul data-tight="true" class="tight">
<li>
<p>Collaboration: Students work together on real‑world environmental challenges,
learning to listen to different perspectives and communicate effectively
to reach shared solutions.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Critical Thinking: They examine how environmental systems work, weigh
evidence, and consider different approaches to sustainability.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Creativity and Adaptability: Students design innovative solutions, from
new ideas for reducing waste to creative ways of conserving resources.</p>
</li>
<li>
<p>Resilience and Confidence: In an assessment‑free environment, students
are encouraged to think boldly and explore new ideas without fear of failure.
This helps them grow more adaptable, confident, and ready to face future
challenges.</p>
</li>
</ul>
<p>Global Literacy: Students become aware of sustainability issues around
the world, such as climate change, resource use, and waste management.
They learn to see connections between local actions and global impact and
understand their role as responsible citizens in a shared world.</p>
<p><strong>Recycling Efforts at Angsana</strong>
</p>
<p>At Angsana Primary School, sustainability comes alive through our school
wide recycling programme. Led by our Eco Stewards, all students take part
in meaningful recycling activities that connect classroom learning to everyday
life.</p>
<p><strong>What Students Do</strong>
</p>
<p>• Learn from peers: Eco Stewards guide classmates on how to sort and dispose
of materials correctly and share the importance of recycling through informal
conversations.</p>
<p>• Bring recyclables from home: Students contribute items to the school’s
recycling drive, reinforcing responsibility and care for the environment.</p>
<p>• Apply classroom learning: Recycling activities make sustainability lessons
tangible and practical.</p>
<p>Our efforts are strengthened through collaboration with CORA Environment,
a waste management company that supports the school in implementing recycling
solutions. By making recycling part of daily school life, Angsana nurtures
students who are not only environmentally aware but also confident, responsible,
and ready to contribute to a greener future</p>

<p><strong>Learning for Life Programme (LLP)</strong>
</p>
<p><strong>Nurturing and Developing 21st Century Leaders through Enrichment and Lifeskills (ENL) Programmes</strong>
</p>
<p><strong>Developing Student Leadership Through Sustainability and Beyond</strong>
</p>
<p>At Angsana Primary School, we believe every child has the potential to
be a leader. Through our Learning for Life Programme (LLP), students are
nurtured as active change agents, taking ownership of real initiatives
that make a difference in their school and community. Guided by our school
vision — A Community of Curious Minds and Serving Hearts — we provide opportunities
for students to grow as leaders, build strong relationships, and learn
how to work effectively in teams.</p>
<p><strong>Leadership Through Sustainability</strong>
</p>
<p>Our Eco Stewards and student leaders play key roles in driving sustainability
efforts. They:</p>
<p>• Plan and lead school wide campaigns.</p>
<p>• Educate peers about eco friendly practices.</p>
<p>• Advocate for positive change in fun and meaningful ways.</p>
<p>These authentic roles help students build confidence, communication skills,
and project management abilities. By taking responsibility for environmental
outcomes, they develop a service mindset and see their power to create
real impact.</p>
<p><strong>Leadership Beyond Sustainability</strong>
</p>
<p>While sustainability is a key focus, LLP also develops student leaders
through Leader in Me platform and in these five key areas:</p>
<p>• Citizenship – learning to contribute responsibly to society.</p>
<p>• Self Management and Social Skills – building resilience, empathy, and
teamwork.</p>
<p>• Physical Activities and Sports – fostering discipline, teamwork, and
perseverance.</p>
<p>• Aesthetics – expressing creativity and confidence through the arts.</p>
<p>• Enterprise – developing initiative, innovation, and problem solving
skills.</p>
<p>Through these experiences, our students learn to influence others, manage
projects, and communicate effectively with diverse groups. They grow into
confident leaders who care for the environment, their peers and the wider
community.</p>

<p><strong>The Leader-in-Me Programme</strong>
</p>
<p>We aim for every student to:</p>
<p>• Attain Personal Mastery – develop good habits in self management.</p>
<p>• Work in Teams – learn how to collaborate and support one another.</p>
<p>• Lead Others – acquire the skills to guide and inspire their peers.</p>
<p>To nurture these qualities, we use the Leader in Me framework, based on
the 7 Habits of Happy Kids:</p>
<p>• Habits 1–3 and 7 focus on self leadership and independence.</p>
<p>• Habits 4–6 focus on teamwork and learning to work well with others.</p>
<p>Form Teachers conduct Leader in Me lessons for Primary 1 to 4 students
(2–3 hours each term), focusing on character development and leadership
habits. For Primary 5 and 6, students reflect on their leadership journey
using curated resources on the Student Learning Space (SLS).</p>
<p>The skills cultivated through the Leader in Me programme — such as self
management, teamwork, and leading others — are reinforced in:</p>
<p>• The LIGHT Programme, where students apply leadership in sustainability
projects.</p>
<p>• The Train the Trainer workshops, where selected students guide their
peers and take on mentoring roles.</p>
<p>Through these experiences, our students learn that leadership is not just
about leading others, but also about serving the community and making a
positive impact.</p>

<p><strong>The LIGHT Programme at Angsana Primary School</strong>
</p>
<p>At Angsana Primary School, we are committed to preparing our students
for an ever changing world. To nurture future ready learners, we have introduced
the LIGHT Programme — a refreshed approach to our Applied Learning Programme
(ALP) and Learning for Life Programme (LLP).</p>
<p>The LIGHT programme is a distinctive, school-based initiative designed
to equip students with essential 21st-century competencies and leadership
skills. It serves as a launchpad for students to thrive in a dynamic environment
while fostering meaningful connections, a deep sense of environmental responsibility,
and a commitment to building a more sustainable future. The programme challenges
students to explore real-world issues, particularly in the area of environmental
sustainability, and empowers them to work together to devise innovative
solutions.</p>
<p>What is LIGHT?</p>
<p>LIGHT stands for:</p>
<p>• Leadership</p>
<p>• Innovation</p>
<p>• Green Initiatives</p>
<p>• Humanity</p>
<p>• Transformation</p>
<p></p>
<div class="isomer-image-wrapper">
<img style="width: 100%" height="auto" width="100%" alt="" src="/images/Screenshot_2026_06_03_122345.png">
</div>
<p>The LIGHT logo visually captures the spirit of the programme. At its centre
lies the school crest, placed within a globe—symbolising the global awareness
and agency of our students. This placement represents how learners apply
their knowledge, work with others, and use design thinking to co-create
solutions for environmental issues.</p>
<p><strong>The Hands: Eco-Stewardship at the Core</strong>
</p>
<p>Eco-stewardship is a central pillar of the LIGHT programme. Students are
encouraged to understand their impact on the environment, take meaningful
action to protect resources, and advocate for sustainability in their daily
lives. By fostering a strong environmental conscience, the programme nurtures
responsible and future-ready citizens.</p>
<p>The logo colours are drawn from our school crest, each with a deeper meaning:</p>
<p>• Red – Heart to Care</p>
<p>• Green – Committed to Learn &amp; Eco Stewardship</p>
<p>• Blue – Steadfast to Serve</p>
<p>Together, these elements reflect our commitment to nurturing students
who are self-directed, confident to work together with others, compassionate,
and capable of making meaningful contributions to Singapore and the world.</p>
<p>Through LIGHT, students are challenged to:</p>
<p>• Explore real world issues, especially in the area of environmental sustainability.</p>
<p>• Work collaboratively to devise innovative solutions that make a positive
impact.</p>
<p>• Develop essential lifeskills such as leadership, teamwork, and empathy.</p>
<p>The LIGHT Programme empowers our students to:</p>
<p>• Think critically and creatively.</p>
<p>• Take initiative in addressing challenges that affect their community
and the environment.</p>
<p>• Embody our renewed goals of becoming lifelong learners who show:</p>
<p>o Curiosity, resilience, and a passion for continuous growth</p>
<p>o Empathy, a strong sense of social responsibility and humanity, and eco
stewardship</p>
<p>By integrating ALP and LLP into a unified vision, LIGHT ensures that every
Angsana student is equipped not only with academic knowledge, but also
with the values and skills needed to thrive in a dynamic future.</p>
<p><strong>Design Thinking: A Human-Centred Approach</strong>
</p>
<p>A key feature of the LIGHT programme is the application of Design Thinking—a
human-centred, solution-focused approach to innovation. Students will use
this structured thinking process as they engage with real-world environmental
issues, guiding them through the stages of:</p>
<p>1. Empathising with community and global challenges</p>
<p>2. Defining the problems they aim to address</p>
<p>3. Ideating possible solutions</p>
<p>4. Prototyping their ideas</p>
<p>5. Testing and refining them through feedback</p>
<p>This empowers students to become creative problem-solvers who can transform
ideas into meaningful action.</p>

<p>Our students embark on a journey of material literacy, stepping back in
time to explore life before plastics became common. This authentic learning
experience helps them connect historical practices of reuse and craftsmanship
to today’s environmental challenges.</p>
<p><strong>Learning Experiences</strong>
</p>
<p>• Students bring pre loved or reused items from home and share why these
objects are meaningful.</p>
<p>• They investigate what the items are made of and interview parents or
grandparents with questions such as:</p>
<p>“What did you use instead of a plastic bag?”</p>
<p>“How did people reuse things instead of throwing them away?”</p>
<p>• In class, they write creative stories about reuse, reflecting on how
habits have changed and how small acts can make a big difference for our
planet.</p>
<p>• They explore Japan’s mottainai spirit — a cultural value of avoiding
waste and finding new uses for every material, such as transforming old
kimonos into futon covers or bags.</p>
<p><strong>The programme nurtures:</strong>
</p>
<p>• Critical and adaptive thinking – comparing traditional and modern materials,
analysing how consumption habits have changed.</p>
<p>• Empathy and communication – interviewing family members to uncover age
old sustainability practices, then personifying items in creative storytelling
to advocate for reuse.</p>
<p>• Teamwork and confidence – collaborating with peers to share findings
and present solutions.</p>
<p>• Leadership development – selected eco stewards take on a “Train the
Trainer” role, assisting teachers in workshops and guiding classmates.</p>
<p>• Sustainability awareness – reflecting on how individual choices at home
can reduce the school community’s environmental footprint.</p>
<p>Students engage in a tangible investigation into the transition from traditional
craftsmanship to the Age of Plastics. By analysing personal pre loved items
and interviewing relatives, teachers, and community stakeholders, they
synthesize diverse perspectives on sustainability. This real world relevance
is deepened through cultural studies and creative adoption projects, where
students blend environmental advocacy with empathetic communication.</p>
<p>Through the humanity and green initiative pillars of LIGHT, our students
learn that every object has a story and every small choice can help build
a greener tomorrow. They take ownership of their learning, curating their
own narratives and persuading the community to adopt sustainable practices.
In doing so, they embody the school’s vision of nurturing curious minds
and serving hearts, while developing the resilience, empathy, and eco stewardship
needed to thrive in an ever changing world.</p>

<p>As part of their post PSLE experience, our Primary 6 students take on
the role of sustainability mentors, stepping into the shoes of their juniors
to understand the challenges they face in adopting eco friendly habits.
This marks a transition from investigating historical narratives in earlier
years to actively solving contemporary problems within the school community.</p>
<p><strong>Learning Experiences</strong>
</p>
<p>• Students begin with empathy building, designing questionnaires and conducting
interviews with Primary 4 peers to uncover authentic barriers to sustainable
practices.</p>
<p>• They sharpen inquiry skills by analysing raw data collected from interviews
and surveys, then defining real world problems.</p>
<p>• Using the Design Thinking process, they move from empathy to ideation,
brainstorming creative solutions such as digital games, video campaigns,
and interactive projects that make sustainability fun and meaningful.</p>
<p>• Students exercise agency and autonomy by designing their own research
tools and choosing the platforms for their final prototypes.</p>
<p><strong>The programme cultivates:</strong>
</p>
<p>• Leadership and teamwork – students collaborate in groups, take responsibility
for project lifecycles, and some step into peer facilitator roles to guide
others.</p>
<p>• Communication and civic literacy – presenting solutions to younger peers
and advocating for sustainable actions.</p>
<p>• Resilience and adaptability – iterating prototypes based on peer feedback
and refining ideas to meet real needs.</p>
<p>• Empathy and responsibility – understanding the perspectives of younger
students and designing age appropriate solutions that inspire change.</p>
<p>• Creative problem solving – transforming complex sustainability concepts
into engaging, practical tools for habit forming.</p>
<p>Authenticity is established through <strong>direct interaction with Primary 4 students</strong>.
By investigating actual barriers within the school community, P6 students
ensure their prototypes are not just classroom exercises but practical
tools designed to solve genuine problems. This progression moves them from
teacher led discovery to autonomous creation, empowering them to transform
P4’s material awareness into P6’s tangible social impact.</p>
<p>The project bridges the gap between different levels of the school, reinforcing
long term sustainability goals by influencing the habits of the next generation.
